I made a soldering iron fume extractor to protect me from harmful gases released during soldering as flux melts. I took an old Foxconn DC brushless fan from an old computer that takes in 12 V, 0.24 A, and got it a wall adapter that outputs 12 V, 1 A. With simple wiring, I connected the fan's positive and negative wires to a DC female plug that connects to the wall adapter. Then I soldered everything together and put heat shrinks on. So now it sucks air away from my face. In addition, I made it a 3d printed frame with AI so that it sucks as much air as possible, and you can put a charcoal-activated filter in it to filter all bad fumes rather than just redirect them from your face. However, I haven't printed it myself because I don't have a 3d printer yet, but I am sure the model is correct.

Now the 3d frame consists of 3 parts. The hood, body tube, and back plate. The body tube is where the charcoal-activated filter sits, and the fan is placed directly behind it, so airflow goes through the filter first. Then the hood is placed onto the body tube by sliding it on, then pressing a bit to flex the plastic to create friction and stay rigid to each other. The hood is designed to grab as much airflow as possible to eliminate all danger; then it passes it to the filter, then to the fan because of the fan suction. The back plate is what holds the fan in place, using 4 bosses in each corner, which the heat inserts should go into so that you can screw screws from the fan to the inserts to keep it in place. In addition, there is a barrel jack hole on the side to provide access for the power. Furthermore, the back plate has a circular hole in the center for the middle of the fan and many other vents on the sides for the blades. The hole setup stands on 2 legs, one on the hood component and one on the body tube. Lastly, to change the filter every once in a while, you pull off the hood, change the filter, then place the hood back. For printing, you should print each part separately and use PETG; I think it is the easiest and most appropriate filament. However, I personally haven't printed it; I just made the design and 3d model with AI, and I am sure it's correct, but because I have no 3d printer, I didn't print it.
