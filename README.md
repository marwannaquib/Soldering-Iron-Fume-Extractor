I made a soldering iron fume extractor to help protect me from harmful gases that are released during the soldering process through flux being melted. I took an old Foxconn DC brushless fan from an old computer that takes in 12 V, 0.24 A, and got it a wall adapter that outputs 12 V, 1 A. With simple wiring, I connected the fan's positive and negative wires to a DC female plug that connects to the wall adapter. Then I  soldered everything together and put heat shrinks on. So now it sucks air away from my face. In addition, I made it a 3d printed frame with AI that you can add a charcoal-enabled filter and a HEPA filter  into to eliminate all dangers, but I haven't physically made it.

Now the 3d frame consists of 3 parts. The hood, body tube, and the back plate. Now the body tube is where the charcoal-activated filter sits, and behind it exactly the fan is placed, meaning airflow goes through the filter first. Then the hood is placed onto the body tube by sliding it on, then pressing a bit to flex the plastic to create friction and stay rigid to each other. The hood is designed to grab as much airflow as possible to eliminate all danger; then it passes it to the filter, then to the fan because of the fan suction. The back plate is what holds the fan in place, using 4 bosses in each corner, which heat inserts should go into so that u can screw screws from the fan to the inserts to keep it in place. In addition, there is a barrel jack hole on the side to open a place for the power. Furthermore, the back plate has a circular hole in the center for the middle of the fan and many other vents on the sides for the blades. Lastly, to change the filter every once in a while, you pull off the hood, change the filter, then place the hood back. For printing, you should print each part on its own and use PETG; I think if is the easiest and most appropriate.
